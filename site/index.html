Free party games for streamers (and couches)

Your stream is the game. Chat holds the cards.

GrabBag.gg puts a party game on your stream. Viewers scan a code, pick a name, and play along on their phones. It's free. No app, no account, and nobody has to dig a controller out from behind the couch.

Made for "wait, rewind that" moments

The best part of a party game isn't the rules. It's the answer nobody saw coming, the long pause while the judge decides, and chat typing "LMAOOO" forty times in a row. GrabBag.gg is built to put those moments on your stream.

Chat plays, not just watches

Your viewers write the answers on their phones, and those answers show up big on your stream. You just get to react.

Lurkers get a job

Not in the game this round? Vote anyway. Everyone watching can pick their favorite from their phone, so the quiet folks get a say too.

Join with a phone

Scan the code on screen, pick a face, type a name, tap Join. Nothing to download and no account to make.

Drops right into OBS

The game screen is a web page. Add it to OBS like any other browser source, or put it on a TV. It looks good on stream without any fiddling.

Run the show from your phone

Pick a game, start, pause, stop. All from the phone in your hand, so you stay on camera and keep talking to chat.

Small chat? Perfect.

You don't need a huge audience. Three regulars is a game. In Apples for Humanity, bots can fill the empty spots.

What's in the bag

Two games so far, with more on the way. If you've played Jackbox, you'll pick these up in about one round.

Apples for Humanity

One player is the judge and reads a prompt card out loud. Everyone else plays the funniest card from their hand. The judge picks a winner. Then someone else is the judge, and the grudges begin.

The real reason I was late today: ____.

A very confident goose.My grandma's group chat.I got stuck in a staring contest.

The judge sees the goose and loses it. Goose wins.

  • A new judge every round, so everyone gets a turn in the hot seat
  • Three kinds of cards: classic fill-in-the-blank, a gentler describe-it set, and wildcards players write themselves
  • Every card flips up on the big screen, so the whole stream sees the reveal
  • Bots can fill empty spots when you're short on players

Quick Quips

Everyone gets a prompt and writes their funniest answer. Two answers go head to head on screen, and the room votes for the winner.

Chat votes. It is not close.

  • Comes with a pack of prompts, ready to go
  • Viewers who aren't playing can still vote on every matchup
  • Block words you don't want on screen and keep answers short
  • Great with a couple of friends or a whole chat

Your stream, your rules

The obvious worry: some stranger joins and types something unhinged. Fair. Here's what you can actually do about it, and how to make each game fit your stream.

Kick anyone, anytime

If someone's being a problem, remove them from the game from your phone. The round keeps going without them.

Block the words you don't want

Quick Quips has a banned-words list and a limit on answer length. You probably already have the list.

Pick the cards

Turn card and prompt packs on or off before you play, including all 55 official Cards Against Humanity packs. Keep the edgy ones for late night and leave them out for the family-friendly stream.

Set the pace

Make timers longer so viewers on stream delay can keep up. Or turn them off and move to the next round yourself when you're done laughing.

Decide how much the crowd counts

Choose how many points audience votes are worth. Make chat the kingmaker, or just a fun little bonus.

A line for the next player

When the game is full, extra viewers wait in line and hop in when a spot opens. Until then, they can still vote.

GrabBag.gg is still early, so some of these settings may move around between versions.

Game night in three steps

You'll need a Windows PC that stays on while you play. The first setup takes a few minutes. After that, it's quick.

  1. Start GrabBag.gg on your PC Grab it from GitHub and start it on your streaming PC. The setup guide walks you through it. Leave it running while you play.
  2. Put the game on stream Add the game screen to OBS as a browser source, or put it on a TV or second monitor.
  3. Let chat scan in Viewers scan the QR code on screen with their phones. You pick a game on your phone and hit Start.

Anyone on your Wi-Fi can join right away. For viewers joining from anywhere else, there's a one-time setup with a free tool. You won't need to touch your router, and the setup guide walks you through it step by step.

Not streaming? It's great on the couch too.

Put the game on your TV, and friends play from whatever phones they already have. No extra controllers, no passing the remote around. Just a room full of people yelling "WHO WROTE THAT?"
